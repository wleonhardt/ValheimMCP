## Contradicts or bugs

- Name trust is spoofable (both projects; sure, it is plain logic). Rule: a trusted NAME counts when exactly one online player has it. Real friend offline, impostor joins with the name: count is 1, trusted. Valheim does not make character names unique (about 80% sure; it refuses a second connection of the same platform id, not of the same name). Our `ChatRules.Trust` has the same hole. See idea 3.
- Our chat id may be forgeable (ours, about 70% sure): `UiId` reads the UserId out of the chat RPC payload. The sending client builds that struct. See idea 1. His parser avoids part of this by resolving the id from the roster by name, which then depends on the unique-name rule.
- His `ChatLines.Parse` reads the rendered chat buffer (`<color=orange>Name</color>: <color=#...>text</color>`). Only the text gets `<` `>` replaced; the sender name is concatenated as is. A client-supplied name containing `</color>: <color=#FFEB04FF>` could fake a different sender or a Shout colour (about 60% sure; depends on name filtering on the sender side). Our RPC-field capture does not have this problem; keep it that way and do not parse the buffer.
- His comment says the game split a line mid-word ("each s" + "torey.", 0.3 s apart). Valheim does not split chat on send as far as I know (about 65% sure); it was most likely the other player's bot or mod. His `Unfinished` heuristic (60+ chars ending in a letter, next line lower case) can glue two unrelated lines from one sender; harmless for hints, wrong for meaning. Idea 5 avoids the hold-back.
- `Safety.Machine` flags a human typing "12:30:00" or "a=b and c=d"; `Hard` slash rule `(^|\s)/(?!s\b|w\b)[a-z]+` flags "and /or". Low impact, but they are false positives that lead to a refusal in his bot; for us they are only hints.
- `Players.Read` skips a loading player whose name equals ours (`pi.m_name == me.GetPlayerName()` with no character): a namesake is invisible to the roster while loading. Minor, same family as the namesake trust issue.
- His comment gives shout reach as 70 m. I could not confirm it; I believe a shout reaches every client and the 15 m "normal" distance is the only hearing limit I am sure of. Do not copy the 70.
- Our own: `world_state` (ext/Shared.cs line 243) prints `m_position` of every player-list entry with no source and no check of `m_publicPosition`. See idea 12.
- Our own: `chat_tail` rows carry `"id"` (the speaker's platform id) even though `players` hides Steam ids unless `hubner.exposePeerHosts` is true. Same personal data, two different rules. Either hide `id` unless the flag is on (keep only `trust`), or document it.
